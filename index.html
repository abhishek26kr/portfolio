03 / POINT OF VIEW

I think AI will become
ordinary infrastructure.

“I don't think AI will stay a ‘special skill.’ I think it will become a normal layer of everyday computing.”

A musician, painter, coder, editor, researcher, teacher, student, employee or shopkeeper doesn't need to become a computer scientist to benefit from a computer. I think AI will move in a similar direction.

The interesting question for me isn't whether people will use AI. It's how they will learn to use it well.

04 / COURSE TESTING

I try to break
educational content.

This is the contribution I want to make to AI education: test from the learner side before the learner sees it.

What feels obvious to an expert can be confusing to a beginner.

I look for the exact point where a student may pause, misunderstand a step, lose context, or fail to reproduce a workflow.

01

Clarity

Can a beginner follow it without hidden prerequisites?

02

Practicality

Can the learner actually use it after the lesson?

03

Confusion

Where is the first likely point of hesitation?

04

Tool testing

Does the demonstrated AI workflow behave as described?

05

Relevance

Would a college student realistically care about it?

06

Perspective

Is there a simpler or different way to frame the idea?

MY REVIEW FORMAT USE ON A REAL LESSON
WHAT WORKED

Clear explanation, useful example, easy-to-follow sequence.

WHERE I PAUSED

What assumed prior knowledge or created friction?

WHAT I WOULD CHANGE

Add, remove, reorder or reframe the part that slowed the learner down.

WHY IT MATTERS

The goal is not more content. The goal is less learner friction.
